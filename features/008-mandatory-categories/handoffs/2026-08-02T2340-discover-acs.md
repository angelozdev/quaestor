# discover-acs — handoff summary

## What I did

Discovered 19 acceptance criteria for the mandatory-category rule, then — at
the owner's request, mid-interview — executed the historical backfill the rule
depends on. The feature arrived needing clean data before it could land; it
leaves with clean data.

## The decision that carried the session

**A category belongs to a direction.** Recording income offers only income
categories; recording an expense offers only expense categories.

The owner asked for the industry comparison before deciding (CLAUDE.md requires
it), and the five reference systems disagree with each other:

| System | Categories typed? |
|---|---|
| YNAB | No income categories exist; inflows go to "Ready to Assign", and categorising one *subtracts* from that category's spending |
| Actual Budget | One income group, undeletable |
| Monarch | Three fixed types — Income, Expenses, Transfers |
| Lunch Money | "Treat as income" flag; the category, not the sign, classifies — but any category may be picked |
| Firefly III | Untyped; the expense-only constraint lives on *budgets* instead |

What settled it was a structural finding: **Quaestor's `Category` is Lunch
Money's, field for field** (`is_income`, `exclude_from_budget`,
`exclude_from_totals`) — but Quaestor also has `Transaction.type`, which Lunch
Money does not. Two sources of truth for the same fact, and a contradiction
none of the reference systems can express. Production showed the owner has been
resolving it by hand for seven months: 467 categorised movements, 0
contradictions. The decision makes the app hold a line the owner already holds.

The consequence that made it urgent: feature 003 computes funding rules from
three-month category averages. One salary of $6.223.101 filed under an expense
category drives that average negative and configures the fund wrong, silently.

## The backfill (AC-19)

The owner interrupted the interview to ask for it directly — *"lo podemos hacer
de hecho ahora mismo"*. Ran a fresh `just backup` first per ADR-0030
(`quaestor-local-2026-08-02.dump`), then worked the data interactively.

**131 uncategorised movements resolved. 101 of them in one pass**, because they
had been produced by the 10 recurring items that carried no category — setting
those 10 cascaded. The remaining 30 were classified one at a time; the `notes`
field answered four of the eight hardest.

```
expense      549 movements   0 uncategorised
income        50 movements   0 uncategorised
transfer      39 movements  39 uncategorised   ← correct, this is AC-3
recurring     14 items       0 uncategorised
direction contradictions:    0
```

Seven categories were created that had no equivalent before: 🎁 Bonos, 💰
Rendimientos, 💳 Cashback (income); 🏦 Comisiones bancarias, 💸 Impuestos
(finance); 🔧 Mantenimiento Carro, 🧽 Lavado Carro (transport). The `4x1000`
charges — Colombia's financial transaction tax, four movements with no category
that fit among 34 — are what forced the inline-creation decision (AC-5) from a
hypothetical into a requirement.

One movement was split by hand: an Amazon charge of US$604,72 that was half a
gift and half personal. **The app cannot split a movement** — the owner asked
for it and it does not exist. Parked.

## Scope drift, resolved

**Transfer categories.** The rule in `feature.md` says a transfer carries none.
The research says Monarch ships a Transfer category type (excluded from budgets
and cash flow), Lunch Money recommends a category with both exclusions on, and
Firefly applies categories to transfers freely — *no reference system forbids
it*. And the owner had already hand-built exactly that: `🔄 Payment / Transfer`,
`exclude_from_budget` and `exclude_from_totals` both on, holding 24 movements.

Offered as broaden / park / drop. Owner chose **park** — a separate discuss,
together with the loan-tracking workaround that shares its shape. 008 does not
grow.

## Parked (in acs.md, none belongs here)

1. Transfer categories — above.
2. Splitting one movement into two.
3. Duplicate `skipped` charges from the recurring engine — Claro 2026-01-28
   exists as ids 1438 *and* 1563; likewise Tigo, Ubidots, Keystone. Harmless to
   totals, but the engine should not do it. Likely relevant to 007.
4. Negative `amount` values — 16 expenses and 15 transfers, while the model
   documents `amount` as positive cents. Used as refunds.
5. Emoji collisions among the new categories.

## Human action needed?

No. Every decision was the owner's, taken in-session. The one AC proposed
rather than decided — AC-13, refusing duplicate category names, which exists
because inline creation makes near-duplicates easy — was put to him after the
ACs were written and confirmed: *"Sí, va AC-13"*.

## Note for whoever plans this

AC-17 needs a constraint that discriminates by movement type — required for
expense and income, forbidden for transfer — not a blanket NOT NULL. Autonomy
drops to `low` on `backend/src/quaestor/migrations/**` per the manifest, and
ADR-0030 requires a fresh backup before the migration runs. AC-18's precondition
(clean data) is already met, so the migration should assert it, not create it.

AC-4 and AC-15 are the same rule seen from two sides: the form prevents the
mistake by not offering the option, every other way in refuses it. Both need
covering — the form's filtering is not the enforcement.
