# atdd — handoff summary

## What I did

Formalized the 19 ACs as 46 Gherkin scenarios, built the step handlers, and
ran the pipeline red. Then dealt with the collision the rule caused in four
features that are already delivered.

## The spec

46 scenarios, every AC covered, none over-covered. AC-17 gets four because the
rule it pins is asymmetric — expense and income require a category, a transfer
requires the absence of one, and the fourth scenario exists so nobody "fixes"
the constraint into a blanket NOT NULL and breaks 39 transfers.

Two phrasings sit lower than the rest of the suite and were checked
deliberately with spec-guardian:

- **AC-17** — `money going out with no category is forced into the records,
  bypassing the app`. This is the only AC in the feature not observed at a
  surface a person uses. It is deliberate: the guarantee has to survive the
  app being circumvented. Accepted as domain language ("the records" is the
  books, not a table name), with the boundary shift now stated in the spec
  header instead of left implicit.
- **AC-18/19** — `the upgrade is attempted` / `the upgrade is refused`.
  Accepted on feature 005's precedent (`AC-12`, same construction). 008's is
  the stronger of the two: the refusal is something the owner actually sees,
  and it names how many movements are left.

## The collision with the delivered features

The rule does not land in isolation. Four suites already describe recording
money without saying what for:

| Suite | Actions that named no category |
|---|---|
| 002 transactions-crud | 15 |
| 005 fx-read-time | 1 |
| 006 planned-payments | 7 |
| 007 recurring-engine | 12 |

And 002 asserted the opposite of AC-11 outright, in a scenario named
`The category can be cleared`.

Put to the owner as a choice: name the category in the spec text, or leave the
text alone and let the handlers invent one. He chose the text — *"Ponerles
categoría en el texto"*. The specs now say what the app actually does.

The line I drew inside that answer, and it is worth checking at CP4: **an
action gets an explicit category; a background `Given` does not.** `When the
user registers an expense … in category "Comida"` is the behaviour under test
and must name it. `Given a recorded expense of 10.00 USD` only needs a
movement to exist — naming a category there would be noise in scenarios that
pin FX conversion. Each amended header now says the `Given` movement *is*
filed under a category the scenario does not name, so the silence cannot be
read as "uncategorised".

`The category can be cleared` was removed from 002 and its AC-3 now holds one
scenario. 008's AC-11 replaces it.

## Note for whoever plans this

`acceptance/handlers/mandatory_categories.py`'s module docstring lists the
exact service surface the red scenarios are calling. That list is the
implementation contract:

1. `record_expense/record_income(..., category_id=None)` must refuse.
2. `categories.list_categories(session, is_income=<bool>)` — the
   direction-filtered offering.
3. `record_expense/record_income` must refuse a category of the wrong
   direction.
4. `record_expense/record_income(..., new_category="<name>")` — inline
   creation in one action, refusing duplicates and offering to restore an
   archived match.
5. `transactions.transfer(..., category_id=...)` must refuse a non-None value.
6. `plan_payment(..., category_id=None)` and
   `create_recurring(..., category_id=None)` must refuse.
7. `update_transaction(tx_id, category_id=None)` must refuse on a movement
   that already carries one.
8. A stored-data constraint that discriminates by movement type.
9. An Alembic revision after `0009` that refuses while any movement is
   uncategorised, naming the count and the kind.

Autonomy drops to `low` on `backend/src/quaestor/migrations/**`, and ADR-0030
requires a fresh backup before that revision touches real data.

## Left alone on purpose

have nothing to do with categories. Not fixed — amending a delivered spec for
reasons outside 008's scope is scope creep. Worth a consolidation task:

1. `005:195` — `## AC-13 — REST and MCP surfaces stay in parity`. The clearest
   leak in the corpus: two protocol names in a heading, sitting above
   scenarios that say "the app and the assistant" correctly.
2. `005:202` — `the assistant interface lists the same expense`; 006 and 007
   both say plainly "the assistant".
3. `007:615/622/628` — `as made by the engine`. "The engine" is this
   subsystem's codename, not an actor the owner meets. The observable is
   "recorded automatically".
4. `002:331` — `Given the user has no session` / `When they request the
   transaction list`.
5. `006:491,500` — the only `Given` in the corpus that instructs the
   implementation to break something.
6. `007:627` — `registers an expense … to "Tienda"` where the rest of the
   corpus says `paying "Tienda"`. Two shapes, two regexes, one action.
