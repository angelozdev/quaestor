## The missing claims, ranked by what rides on them

None of these is a defect. The code is right today — CP7 drove it and this sweep
found nothing that moves a figure. What follows is what no test would notice if
it stopped being right. Each is stated as the claim, not the test.

**1 — A fund filling for a charge that arrives every month says its category
still has repeating charges.** The True side of `has_repeating_charges` is
asserted nowhere in Python; the one assertion that exists asserts False. With
the mutant in place every fund whose breakdown is empty tells the owner *«La
categoría ya no tiene cobros recurrentes … Bórralo»* — an instruction to delete
a fund that is working. This is the single most dangerous survivor: it is one
line, it is the field CP7 already flagged as unguarded (§4.4), and 1824 Python
tests stay green through it.

**2 — A category holding a monthly charge beside a yearly one is offered the
rule that saves for repeating charges.** `has_something_to_spread` has no Python
assertion at all. Inverted, the create form withdraws the subscription rule from
exactly the categories this feature exists to unblock — 🛡️ Auto Insurance and
🔥 Services are both a monthly charge beside a yearly one. AC-14's two scenarios
are vitest and hand-set the field, so the only guard on the service that
computes it is the absence of one.

**3 — A fund whose only charge stopped repeating in an earlier month, with
nothing skipped and nothing paid this month, says the category has no repeating
charge left.** CP7's fix number four is guarded by one scenario whose charge has
a turn *this month* that is skipped. That shape survives the mutation
identically (`real=True mutant=True` above); the defect lives in the shape with
no turn at all, which no scenario has. AC-8's sentence would return where AC-9's
is true, which is the precise defect CP7 fixed.

**4 — A category still says it has repeating charges when one of several rules
has run out and another has not.** The aggregation over obligations
(`_still_charges_in`) is a different predicate from the per-obligation answer in
claim 1, and it is unasserted separately: a live monthly charge beside a
finished one reads as a dead category.

**5 — A charge that arrives every two months and lands next month is announced
before the fund is created, and its category is offered the rule.** The
spreadable boundary is pinned from below — `> 1` becoming `> 0` dies on rung 2,
because a monthly charge would then warn and AC-13 goes red — and not from
above, because every cadence in every scenario is either monthly (1) or yearly
(12). Nothing occupies the middle, so `> 3` is indistinguishable from `> 1` and
a bimonthly or quarterly charge can lose its warning entirely.

**6 — A fund that already holds all but one peso of a charge with no month to
spread over still announces the peso.** CP7's fix number three is pinned only at
exactly zero. Two cents of exposure, listed last because it is last, but it is
the same predicate the fix introduced and it costs one number in an existing
scenario to close.
