# atdd — handoff summary

## What I did

Formalized the 24 approved ACs as 59 standard-Gherkin scenarios in `spec.md`,
wrote the step handlers that bind them to the services and conversational
layers, and ran the pipeline end to end. The generator and runner needed no
change — they are generic over the IR.

## Artifacts produced

- `features/006-planned-payments-to-pay/spec.md` — 59 scenarios, one section
  per AC
- `acceptance/handlers/planned_payments.py` — the feature's step handlers
- `acceptance/handlers/__init__.py` — registers the new handler module
- `acceptance/handlers/world.py` — outstanding-queue scenario state plus a
  `cleanups` list so a scenario that installs a global hook removes it
- `acs.md` / `feature.md` — updated for the AC-24 defect decided this session

## Findings

**A third defect, surfaced by the run itself (AC-24).** The screen's headline
figure is the combined amount owed — 130.000 for an 85.000 overdue bill plus a
45.000 upcoming one. The conversational answer prints 85.000 under one heading
and 45.000 under the other and never states 130.000, so the same question
answered two ways gives the user different work. Angelo decided the
conversational answer must state the combined total; AC-24 and the scope now
say so, and the scenario stays red until it does.

**Red set is exactly the decided target behaviour.** 6 of 60 executions fail:
three restore-a-skip scenarios (AC-8, `planned.restore_payment` does not
exist), two income-exclusion scenarios (AC-15, planned incomes still reach the
queue and inflate the total), and the assistant-total scenario (AC-24). Every
other scenario passes against shipped code, which is the evidence that the
handlers bind to the real system rather than to stubs.

**Two spec bugs found and fixed before approval.** Thirteen scenarios viewed
the outstanding list without setting an exchange rate and failed on AC-20's
fail-loud contract rather than on what they were specifying; the inverted-period
scenario had the same problem. Both now set a rate in their preconditions —
the queue surface genuinely requires one.

**Handler note for later checkpoints.** `world.cleanups` exists because the
AC-21 scenario installs a failing post-confirm step globally; without the undo
it would leak into every scenario that ran after it in the same session.
