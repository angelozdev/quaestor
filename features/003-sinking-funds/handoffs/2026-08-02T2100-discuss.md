# discuss — handoff summary

## What I did

Resumed the parked `003-sinking-funds` redesign. The session turned on one
question the owner kept circling: *are goals and sinking funds the same thing?*

Answered it from three directions — the code, the industry, and the owner's own
production data — and the three agreed. Promoted 003 to `ready` and split a
prerequisite out as `008-mandatory-categories`.

## Findings

**The goal/fund confusion had a concrete cause.** Reading the models settled
what the two mechanisms actually are: an envelope is `category × month ×
amount` with rollover and no money movement; a goal *requires* a savings account
(validated: must exist, must be type `savings`, must not be archived) and each
contribution writes a real two-leg internal transfer. They share an intent and
share no machinery. The owner's own description of what a goal *should* be —
*"no está asociado a ninguna cuenta, simplemente baja el monto que puedo
gastar"* — is the definition of the envelope, not of what the app shipped.

**Goals are invisible to the budget.** `safe_to_spend_calc` subtracts committed
recurring expenses, planned expenses, assigned envelopes, unbudgeted spending
and overspend. A goal contribution is a `transfer`, and `_committed` filters to
`TxType.expense`. Goals appear in no term of the headline, before or after
contributing.

**Industry check (CLAUDE.md requires it before recommending an architecture).**
YNAB and Actual Budget — both envelope budgeters — ship *no* goals feature; a
goal is one target type among several on a category, with no account involved.
Monarch does bind goals to accounts and derives progress from account balances,
but Monarch is a net-worth tracker, not an envelope budgeter. Quaestor is an
envelope budgeter (product ADR-002) carrying a Monarch-shaped goal — the
mismatch is the defect.

**Production proved it better than the argument did** (read-only queries
against the local Postgres):

- **0 budget rows in the app's entire history.** Nothing to migrate; the
  migration risk in the original park note is void.
- **1 goal, 0 contributions.** Goal "Korea" reports `$0 of $10.000.000` while
  the `🇰🇷 Korea` savings account it was forced to link holds **$14.659.572** —
  surpassed by 46%, deadline 2026-08-31, and the app cannot tell. Three
  proposed transfers sit unconfirmed. The current design pays Monarch's cost
  (mandatory account) and collects none of its benefit (account-derived
  progress).
- **The lumpiness is large and real:** $7.000.000 annual car insurance +
  $447.300 SOAT = $620.608/month of smoothing that today lands as one crater;
  Ubidots Bonus US$2.847 quarterly = US$949/month normalized.
- **7 months / 477 posted expenses** — the `average` rule is viable today.
- **10 of 14 active recurring items carry no category**, which caps
  `from-recurring` at 3 derivable funds instead of 8. This became 008.

**Corrected a wrong diagnosis mid-session.** I attributed the zero-envelope
history to the design having failed the owner, and warned the new system risked
the same fate. The owner corrected it: they were waiting to accumulate enough
history to set a meaningful average. That reframing is what made "the app starts
empty" a defensible choice rather than a repeat of the failure.

## Decisions

1. Goals become funds. One machine, four rules: `fixed` | `average` |
   `from-recurring` | `target-by-date`. The goals screen, tables and
   month-close contribution hook are removed.
2. The rule *is* the number — no monthly assign ritual (unlike YNAB/Actual,
   which both require a monthly distribute action).
3. The new headline replaces the old outright and expands into a visible
   breakdown, rather than running in parallel with it.
4. A fund holding money already gets its opening balance typed once at
   creation; it never tracks an account afterwards.
5. **The app starts empty — the user creates each fund.** Owner's call,
   reversing the recommendation on the table (auto-propose from recurring items
   + history). Auto-proposal remains available later if adoption stalls.
6. `goal-contribution-hooks` dies — which unblocks consolidation task 4
   (`month-close-rollover`), whose scope caveat was waiting on this feature.
7. Rule choice is per-category correct, not interchangeable: `Auto Insurance`
   averages to a wrong $149.100 vs a correct $620.608 `from-recurring`.
8. `average` carries a known feedback trap (overspend raises the budget);
   Actual's guidance adopted — average for the uncontrollable, fixed for the
   capped.
