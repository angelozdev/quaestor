# atdd — handoff summary

## What I did

Formalised the 30 approved ACs as standard Gherkin in
`features/003-sinking-funds/spec.md`, generated the pipeline, and wrote the step
handler module that binds its vocabulary to the services layer this feature will
build.

## The suite

**92 scenarios, 612 steps** — the largest in the project by half again.

| | 006 | 007 | 008 | **003** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| scenarios | 59 | 70 | 46 | **92** |

That is a finding, not a boast. `feature.md` already warned this feature does
not fit one pipeline run, and the spec is the first artefact to put a number on
it. The phasing decision stays where it belongs, at Checkpoint 4.

## A pure red phase

Every one of the 92 is red, and none of them was ever expected to pass. This is
unlike 008, where AC-6, AC-8, AC-9 and AC-19 ran green from the start because
the recurring engine already did what they asked. Here nothing exists: no fund,
a different formula behind the number this replaces, and goals still their own
concept.

The reds come in exactly two shapes, by design:

```
there is no funds service yet — a fund is the noun this whole feature adds
the assistant has no 'create_fund' tool yet — AC-28 asks for full parity
```

Each handler names the capability it wanted, so the red output reads as a to-do
list. The eight the module names are `create_fund`, `preview_fund`,
`list_funds`, `set_fund`, `delete_fund`, `fund_status`, `available` and
`rates`. **Those are the intent, not the design** — Checkpoint 4 owns their
shape, the same way 007's CP3 named five APIs it did not design.

## A departure from the other suites, stated on purpose

This spec pins **absolute calendar dates** (`Given today is 2026-11-10`) where
002, 005, 006 and 007 all prefer relative ones. Every fund rule is month
arithmetic, and "2 months ago" cannot pin $74.550 the way a fixed calendar can.
The clock step is new vocabulary and it is the reason the arithmetic in the spec
is checkable at all.

## Three fixture bugs of mine, and one that changed the spec after approval

The first red run was dirty — 30 of the failures were my own setup errors, not
target behaviour. Worth recording because the third one is not cosmetic.

1. **50 scenarios used an account they never declared.** `_declare` resolves a
   missing account to a sentinel id and the obligation is refused, so the red
   said "account not found" instead of "no funds service". Fixed by declaring
   the account in every scenario that uses one, matching 007's house style.
2. **35 scenarios named an income category that was never created.** Same
   shape: the red was `category 999999321 not found`.
3. **Three scenarios declared a repeating income as `waiting for approval`,
   which feature 007 forbids outright** — its AC-6 rules that recurring incomes
   are always automatic, because expected money never enters the to-pay queue.
   Two were rewritten with no loss of meaning. The third could not be: AC-15's
   first scenario was *"An income that never arrives is not noticed by the
   app"*, and with an automatic income "never arrives" is not expressible. It is
   now *"An income that has not arrived is never flagged by the app"*, asserting
   the same thing AC-15 actually claims — the app raises nothing on its own —
   from a date before the income is due.

**That third change landed after Angelo approved the spec.** It is small and it
is forced by an accepted rule of another feature rather than chosen, but it is a
semantic change to a scenario he had already read, so it is flagged here rather
than absorbed silently.

## What this checkpoint deliberately did not decide

The three items `acs.md` left for the spec are still open, and two of them
turned out to belong further down the pipeline than CP3:

- **The Spanish wording of each refusal.** Tangled with consolidation C9 — every
  domain refusal reaches the toast in English today, against charter §3. The
  spec asserts that a refusal *says the right thing*, matching on the word that
  carries the meaning, so the wording can be settled without touching the spec.
- **Whether funds get their own screen or replace the budgets screen.** No
  scenario depends on it.
- **The shape of the breakdown when many funds exist.** The spec asserts the
  breakdown names each fund and adds up, and states explicitly that order is not
  pinned.
