# discover-acs — handoff summary

## What I did

Interviewed the owner through the four discovery passes and wrote
`features/003-sinking-funds/acs.md` — 28 acceptance criteria for the feature
that replaces envelopes, goals and safe-to-spend with a single machine.

Greenfield mode: 003 has no `spec.md`, and although it deletes shipped code, the
behaviour being specified is new.

Every question was framed with figures read from the production Postgres
(read-only, ADR-0030) rather than hypotheticals — the owner's stated preference,
and the reason two errors in `feature.md` surfaced during discovery rather than
during implementation.

## Findings

**Two figures in `feature.md` do not survive contact with production.** It
claims 8 funds become derivable once 008 lands; the real count is 7 expense
categories. And it claims 🌐 Internet "resolves to its exact $85.000"; Internet
holds **three** recurring items totalling $161.400. The second error mattered
more than the first — `feature.md` describes `from-recurring` as a "linked
recurring item", singular, while the data shows several per category is the
normal case (Internet ×3, Auto Insurance ×2, Services ×2 with *different*
intervals). That is AC-4, and it would not have been asked without the query.

**Four decisions were settled by research, not assertion**, as CLAUDE.md
requires. Actual Budget's primary docs confirmed that a dated obligation divides
by the months remaining and recalculates — not a fixed ÷12 — and that Actual is
deliberately permissive about thin history. YNAB's documentation confirmed the
opposite posture on income: budget only what is already in the account. YNAB
also turned out to have **no average target type at all** — its "Average Spent"
is a one-time auto-assign suggestion, never a standing rule — which matters
because Quaestor's decision 2 removes the monthly ritual that suggestion lives
in. Actual is therefore the only precedent for `average` as a live rule.

**One decision was taken over my objection, and I recorded both.** I showed the
owner that smoothing an expense forward is conservative while smoothing income
forward is optimistic, and that YNAB forbids the second outright. The owner
chose to smooth anyway, with a reason that answers the objection rather than
dismissing it: a recurring income is a *declaration* of what you count on, and
the failure case is an edit, not a detection. That reasoning is what produced
AC-15, and it is why the app deliberately does not try to notice a missed
payment. Decision 5 in `acs.md` states the objection and the answer side by side
so a reviewer can weigh it.

**I got the Korea goal wrong and the owner corrected me.** I called it "already
met" from the $14.659.572 sitting in the linked savings account. The owner's
reply — *"olvida la cuenta"* — is this feature's own thesis: the account is not
the goal's progress. The $10.000.000 exist but were never registered, and the
owner is waiting for this feature to register them. My framing was wrong, not
the answer.

**The owner reversed himself once, correctly.** He first excluded the assistant
("no lo uso"), then asked whether including it was actually cheaper. It is, and
I verified rather than guessed: the seven goal tools are deleted either way,
`set_budget` is the very tool funds replace, the rules live in `services/` so
the tools inherit every refusal free — 008's AC-14 needed no code for exactly
this reason — and skipping parity would have required an ADR overriding charter
§2. Parity is in, as AC-28.

**A defect this feature closes for free.** Consolidation **C14** — an envelope
can be assigned to an income category today, permanently depressing the headline
with no way to clear it — is the same refusal as AC-22, on the surface that
replaces it.

## What is deliberately not decided here

Three items were pushed to Checkpoint 3 rather than absorbed: the exact Spanish
wording of each refusal (tangled with consolidation C9, where every domain
refusal reaches the toast in English), whether funds get their own screen or
replace the budgets screen, and the shape of the headline's breakdown when many
funds exist.

## Human action needed?

**Yes — review.** Every decision in `acs.md` is Angelo's, taken across seven
question rounds. But the assembled document has not been read by him, and the AC
contract is the human's to sign. Checkpoint 3 does not start until it is.

Two things in it deserve a deliberate look rather than a skim:

- **AC-14 and decision 5** — smoothing irregular income, taken over a stated
  objection. If the reasoning does not hold up on a second read, this is the
  cheapest moment to change it.
- **AC-16** — nothing is frozen, so past months rewrite themselves. This is
  already how the shipped code behaves and it is where Quaestor departs from
  both YNAB and Actual, where an assignment is a stored fact.

## Recommended next step

Approve or amend `acs.md`, then `/engineer.atdd` for Checkpoint 3.

Two things for whoever plans this: `feature.md` already warns the feature is too
large for one pipeline run, and nothing in these ACs contradicts that. And AC-27
deletes real production records, so it runs behind a fresh `just backup` and
explicit human authorisation (charter §7, ADR-0030).
